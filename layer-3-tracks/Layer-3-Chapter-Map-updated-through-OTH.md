## Basis and constraints

Layer 3 is the **discipline-track layer**. It assumes the mathematical substrate in Layer 1 and the shared core-engineering material in Layer 2; it is not intended to reteach those chapters.

The project source establishes seven FE routes, while the FE Reference Handbook 10.6 contains **six dedicated discipline sections**: Chemical Engineering (p. 243), Civil Engineering (p. 265), Environmental Engineering (p. 318), Electrical and Computer Engineering (p. 361), Industrial and Systems Engineering (p. 422), and Mechanical Engineering (p. 436). The guide also explicitly establishes that **Other Disciplines has no dedicated Handbook discipline section**; that route draws from the general sections and its exam specification.

The Handbook and appendix specification establish subject areas, not this chapter-by-chapter breakdown. Therefore, the chapter titles, ranges, and counts below are **project-planning decisions** designed to:

- add discipline-specific depth without duplicating Layer 1 or Layer 2 ownership,
- preserve one global Layer 3 chapter sequence,
- align each track with the corresponding Handbook discipline section and FE specification,
- keep concept ownership in the ledger authoritative,
- and leave `03-96` through `03-99` unassigned for future bridge, capstone, or specification-change needs.

The current ledger tooling already recognizes Layer 3 concept-domain prefixes `CHE`, `CIV`, `ECE`, `ENV`, `IND`, `MEC`, and `OTH`. These are **track/domain prefixes**, not Layer 2-style tier letters.

## Build-order intent

Layer 3 is **route-selective**, unlike Layer 2. A candidate should normally build only the discipline track required by the selected FE exam, plus any explicitly identified cross-track prerequisite.

Within a track, drafting should proceed in chapter order unless the concept ledger identifies a prerequisite reason to reorder. The concept ledger—not the chapter map—remains the authority for:

- concept ownership,
- prerequisite edges,
- `uses_terms` / `uses_symbols` dependencies,
- Handbook verification state,
- specification mappings,
- and figure ownership.

Before any Layer 3 chapter is drafted, its concept atoms should be registered in the ledger with `layer: 3`, the appropriate `track`, an approved domain prefix, and explicit prerequisites back into Layers 1–2 or earlier chapters in the same track.

Planned map entries do **not** count as concept registration, Handbook verification, or figure-manifest registration.

## Cross-track reuse rule

Layer 3 should not create duplicate concept owners simply because two FE specifications mention the same subject. When specialized material is truly shared, the first canonical owner should be reused through prerequisites or route mappings.

Examples:

- Civil and Environmental may share hydrology or groundwater concepts, but the ledger should have one canonical owner for an identical term or method.
- Chemical and Environmental may share reactor or mass-transfer concepts, but discipline-specific applications should depend on—not silently re-own—the shared concept.
- Mechanical and Other Disciplines may share vibrations, failure, HVAC, or compressible-flow concepts; the Other Disciplines integration track should preferentially synthesize earlier material rather than duplicate ownership.

## Source boundary

**Source-established facts:** Layer 3 is the discipline-track layer; the guide has seven FE routes; Handbook 10.6 has six dedicated discipline sections beginning on pp. 243, 265, 318, 361, 422, and 436; Other Disciplines has no dedicated discipline section; and the FE appendix provides separate exam specifications for all seven routes.

**Project-planning decisions:** the 95 planned chapter titles, the chapter-count allocation among tracks, the global numbering ranges, the Other Disciplines integration-chapter structure, and the reservation of 03-96 through 03-99.

No Layer 3 concepts, source chapters, or figures are registered merely by creating this map.
