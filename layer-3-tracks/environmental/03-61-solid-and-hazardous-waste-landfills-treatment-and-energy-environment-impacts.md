## 61.1 Municipal solid-waste generation, composition, and mass balance

Solid-waste systems track material by mass and volume through collection, recycling, composting, thermal processing, and disposal. Moisture and density affect volume and energy content.

\[\text{generated}=\text{recycled}+\text{processed}+\text{disposed}+\Delta\text{storage}\]

## 61.2 Collection, transfer, recycling, composting, and waste-to-energy

Solid-waste management is a system problem. High recovery can reduce disposal volume but requires collection, processing, markets, and contamination control.

\[\text{system cost/environmental impact}=f(\text{collection, transfer, processing, recovery, disposal})\]

## 61.3 Landfill liners, leachate, gas, and cover water balance

Modern landfills manage water infiltration, leachate, gas, settlement, and cover performance. The Handbook provides simplified liner breakthrough, gas-flux, and cover-water-balance relationships.

## 61.4 Hazardous-waste compatibility and characterization

Hazardous wastes must be characterized before mixing, storage, transport, or treatment. Incompatibility can cause heat, gas generation, fire, pressure, or toxic releases.

\[\text{characterize}\rightarrow\text{segregate incompatible wastes}\rightarrow\text{select treatment/storage}\]

![FIG-03-61-004: Hazardous-waste compatibility matrix and segregated storage concept.](../figures/FIG-03-61-004-hazardous-waste-compatibility-and-characterization.png)

**Solution.** Apply the relation and environmental model in §61.4; then verify units, boundary conditions, and physical limits.

## 61.5 Hazardous and radioactive waste treatment/disposal

Treatment selection depends on hazard, chemistry, phase, volume, and final disposal requirements. Stabilization can reduce mobility without destroying contaminant mass; thermal treatment can destroy suitable organics.

\[\text{treatment}= \text{physical}+\text{chemical}+\text{biological}+\text{thermal as appropriate}\]

![FIG-03-61-005: Physical, chemical, biological, thermal, stabilization, and secure-disposal pathways for hazardous waste.](../figures/FIG-03-61-005-hazardous-and-radioactive-waste-treatment-disposal.png)

### Worked Example 5

**Problem.** Solidification/stabilization primarily reduces mobility rather than destroying metals.

**Solution.** Apply the relation and environmental model in §61.5; then verify units, boundary conditions, and physical limits.

## 61.6 Energy sources, efficiency, and environmental burdens

Energy technologies differ in greenhouse gases, air emissions, water use, land use, waste, reliability, and lifecycle impacts. Comparisons require a consistent functional basis.

\[\text{impact intensity}=\frac{\text{environmental burden}}{\text{energy delivered}}\]

![FIG-03-61-006: Matrix comparing conventional and alternative energy sources across carbon, air, water, land, waste, and reliability dimensions.](../figures/FIG-03-61-006-energy-sources-efficiency-and-environmental-burdens.png)

### Worked Example 6

**Solution.** Apply the relation and environmental model in §61.6; then verify units, boundary conditions, and physical limits.

## 61.7 Carbon footprint, water-energy links, and lifecycle perspective

Carbon footprint aggregates greenhouse gases on a common CO2-equivalent basis. Energy production also affects thermal discharges and water withdrawal/consumption; lifecycle boundaries must be stated.

\[\mathrm{CO_2e}=\sum m_i\,GWP_i\]

![FIG-03-61-007: Energy lifecycle from resource extraction through conversion, transmission/use, emissions, water use, waste, and end-of-life.](../figures/FIG-03-61-007-carbon-footprint-water-energy-links-and-lifecycle-perspective.png)

**Solution.** Apply the relation and environmental model in §61.7; then verify units, boundary conditions, and physical limits.

## Where This Goes Wrong

**Using solid waste mass balance without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using solid waste management system without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using landfill system without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using hazardous waste compatibility without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using hazardous waste treatment without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using energy-source environmental impact without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Using environmental lifecycle impact without defining the environmental basis.** Confirm the control volume, constituent/media, units, time scale, and assumptions before solving.

**Confusing concentration with mass loading.** Always check whether the requested quantity is mass/volume or mass/time.

**Ignoring residual streams or transferred pollution.** Treatment often moves mass from water to sludge, air, spent media, or another phase rather than destroying it.

**Treating every required topic as a Handbook lookup.** The FE Environmental specification includes learned concepts that are not fully tabulated in Handbook 10.6.
