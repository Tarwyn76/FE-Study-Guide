## On the Board Today

The Handbook includes definitions and enforcement provisions from the 2025–26 NCEES Model Law. These are model provisions: actual licensure is granted and regulated by each jurisdiction. For FE purposes, you need to understand the structure—what practice of engineering means, what responsible charge requires, how titles and seals are controlled, and why jurisdiction-specific law remains authoritative.

## 02.1 Professional Engineer, Engineer Intern, and the Board

The Model Law defines a Professional Engineer as an individual qualified by education, training, experience, and examination and duly licensed by the board. An Engineer Intern is an individual certified as such by the board.

The licensing board is the jurisdictional authority that administers the applicable engineering law and rules. NCEES develops model documents and examinations; it does not itself issue a state engineering license.

For exam questions, keep titles straight: passing the FE is an important step, but it does not by itself make someone a Professional Engineer.

## 02.2 Practice of Engineering

The Model Law defines practice of engineering broadly as services or creative work requiring engineering education, training, and experience in applying engineering principles and interpreting engineering data to activities that can affect public health, safety, and welfare.

Examples include planning, studies, designs, specifications, technical submissions, commissioning, and review of design products for compliance.

The legal boundary matters because unlicensed practice and misuse of protected titles can trigger disciplinary or enforcement action.

## 02.3 Responsible Charge

Responsible charge means exercising full professional knowledge of and control over the work. The Handbook lists four core elements: authority to review and change or approve work throughout development; personal awareness of scope and limitations; ability to answer questions demonstrating knowledge of the engineering decisions; and acceptance of full responsibility.

Responsible charge is not satisfied by a last-minute signature review. The responsible engineer must be involved sufficiently to direct and control the engineering work.

This concept is central to signing and sealing.

## 02.4 Signatures, Seals, and Technical Responsibility

A signature verifies or authenticates a document; a seal is the professional symbol or information prescribed by law and rule. Licensees should sign and seal only work that conforms to accepted standards and safeguards the public.

They should not sign or seal technical work outside their competence or work not prepared under their responsible charge. For multidisciplinary projects, each technical segment may be signed and sealed by the licensee responsible for that segment.

The exam trap is the ceremonial-signature idea: a PE cannot ethically or legally function as a rubber stamp.

## 02.5 Jurisdiction and Comity

A jurisdiction is the state, District of Columbia, territory, commonwealth, or possession that issues licenses and regulates practice within its legal boundaries.

A license in one jurisdiction does not automatically authorize unrestricted practice in another. Engineers must comply with the laws and rules of every jurisdiction in which they practice. Licensure by comity is a pathway by which a jurisdiction may recognize qualifications from elsewhere, subject to its own requirements.

The Handbook's Model Law is therefore a model, not a substitute for checking the actual board requirements where work is performed.

## 02.6 Disciplinary Action and Unlicensed Practice

The Model Law lists grounds for disciplinary action against licensees and separate grounds for enforcement against unlicensed individuals. Examples include fraud, negligence, incompetence, misconduct, practicing outside competence, violating board law or rules, false statements, and misuse of a license or seal.

For unlicensed individuals, prohibited conduct includes practicing without authorization, falsely presenting oneself as a PE, using another person's seal, impersonating a licensee, or using an expired or revoked license.

The public-protection purpose links discipline back to Chapter 02-01.

## 02.7 Firms, Certificates of Authorization, and Exemptions

The Model Law includes requirements for firms offering engineering services, including certificates of authorization in jurisdictions adopting such provisions. It also defines limited exemptions, including work by employees or subordinates when final engineering decisions remain under the responsible charge and verification of a properly licensed individual.

Do not generalize exemptions beyond the text. Actual state law varies.

The exam-level principle is that firm authorization, individual licensure, competent responsible charge, and jurisdictional compliance are separate requirements that can overlap.

## Practice Problems

1. An engineer passes the FE exam. Are they automatically a Professional Engineer?

2. A PE in one state is asked to offer engineering services in another state where they are not licensed. What must be checked?

3. A PE receives a completed design at the end of a project and is asked to seal it after a brief review. Is that responsible charge?

4. A multidisciplinary project has structural and electrical segments. How should technical responsibility be handled?

5. An unlicensed person advertises themselves as a Professional Engineer. What category of issue is this?

6. A licensee practices outside their demonstrated technical competence. What regulatory concern arises?

7. A firm offers engineering services in a jurisdiction that requires a certificate of authorization. What must the firm verify?

8. An employee performs engineering calculations under a PE. What must remain with the PE under the model exemption concept?

9. A licensee uses an expired seal. What kind of enforcement issue is raised?

10. Why must actual jurisdiction law be checked even though the Handbook includes the Model Law?


---

## Practice Problem Solutions

1. No. FE passage is a step toward intern certification/licensure; PE status requires jurisdictional licensure.

2. The licensing law and authorization requirements of the jurisdiction where services are offered or performed.

3. No. Responsible charge requires meaningful authority, knowledge, review, direction, and responsibility throughout development.

4. Each technical segment should be under the responsible charge of a qualified licensee responsible for that segment, while overall coordination may be separate.

5. Unlicensed practice/misuse of protected title.

6. Grounds for discipline and a competence violation.

7. Whether the jurisdiction requires and has issued the firm's certificate of authorization.

8. Final engineering decisions, responsible charge, and verification.

9. Disciplinary/unlicensed-practice enforcement depending on status; use of an invalid license/seal is prohibited.

10. Model provisions are not the enacted law of every jurisdiction; boards adopt or modify requirements.
